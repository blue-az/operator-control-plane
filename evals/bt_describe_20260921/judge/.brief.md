# Brief — blind assessment of three outsider readings

Three analysts read the same seven captured pages in `./inputs/` and described the system
they found. Their answers are `A.md`, `B.md`, `C.md`. You do not know who wrote which; do
not speculate, and do not name any model.

They were asked for: a mechanical description; a split of demonstrated versus asserted with
passages quoted; what an outsider cannot determine and what would settle it; and the likeliest
misreading the material invites. They were told that "verified" in a caption is not a described
method, to quote both sides where two pages disagree, and not to soften findings.

You are judging fidelity to the captured pages, not truth about the real system. A claim may
be true of the world and still unsupported by this corpus; mark it unsupported. Write
`VERDICT.md` here covering:

1. **Unsupported claims.** For each answer, list anything asserted that the seven pages do not
   say — invented mechanisms, numbers that appear nowhere, capabilities inferred from names.
   Quote the offending line and say which page it purports to rest on. Most important section.
2. **Accuracy of the demonstrated/asserted split.** Sample their claims both ways: something
   they called demonstrated that the pages only assert, and something they called asserted that
   the pages actually evidence. Check each against the source.
3. **Cross-page contradictions.** The pages were captured together and may disagree. Name any
   real contradiction in the corpus, then say which analysts found it and which missed it.
4. **The likeliest misreading.** For each, is the named misreading genuinely invited by the
   quoted text, or manufactured? Is it the strongest one available in this corpus? If a better
   one exists that all three missed, name it and quote it.
5. **Ranking**, best to worst, with reasons and stated confidence.

Judge only what is on the page.
