The seat: gemma4:26b

Tied with gemma4:31b on correctness and statistically inseparable from it, so the decision falls to the two axes that can separate them. It is 1.4× faster (13.2s vs 17.9s median) and reaches 17 of its 24 passes with a clean trajectory against 31b's 12 — half of 31b's successes arrive via a blind patch, a skipped source, or a repeat. Both are perfectly deterministic across 18 trials. Nothing in 1,317 cells justifies the larger model for this work.

Parameter count predicts almost nothing

The most portable result here

A 12B model outranks a 33B one by roughly 300 Elo. The spread within the gemma4 family is comparable to the spread across the entire field. On a separate 90-cell floor sweep the ordering was monotonic in size from 3.4B to 14.8B — but that monotonicity vanishes above ~14B, where family and tuning dominate.

The corollary for hardware: qwen3:32b occupies 20 GB of VRAM to finish sixth of seven. Spending capacity on parameter count is not a strategy on this workload class.

The instrument had to be repaired six times

Every defect first appeared as a model difference

Harness defects found, and what each one faked
DefectWhat it looked like
Tool-call extraction used a greedy brace span A correct call emitted as two JSON objects failed to parse and was returned as prose. Read as two model families having an “emission quirk”.
System prompt gated on a substring of the model name One model silently received a strict-JSON instruction its comparators did not — speaking directly to the behaviour being graded.
No sampling or context control was sent at all Context came from each model’s own defaults, so it differed per model and pushed a 32B model into CPU spill.
Reasoning mode uncontrolled Thinking-capable models ran with reasoning on by default, costing 5–32× the tokens. A 19× apparent latency spread collapsed to 1.2× once controlled.
The loop exited after the first file edit A multi-file task scored 0 of 42 across seven models. It was structurally unpassable; the same model passes it with a continuation budget.
Scope rule stated but never graded A model could make the required edit and also rewrite unrelated files with no penalty.
The reusable lesson

One fixture was on record as the only one ever producing per-model shape differences. Under a repaired harness it scores 6/6 for all seven models. The apparent capability ladder was substantially artifact. The working rule that came out of this: when a model difference appears, assume the harness until a retained trace proves otherwise.

How much of any result is noise

Measured, not assumed

A dedicated probe ran 142 cells that were provably unaffected by the variable under test, in two separate invocations at identical settings. Aggregate rates differed by 4.2 points — but 16 of 142 individual cells (11.3%) flipped.

That figure bounds everything else here. It explains why single-trial results are untrustworthy, why confidence intervals stayed wide at n=6, and why two adjacent pairs in the ranking cannot be separated by adding trials.

It is also not uniform. Four models reproduced to the decimal across separate invocations; the only two that drifted beyond the floor were precisely the two an independent stability metric had already flagged. Instability is a property of specific models, not a smear across the field — and it is predictable in advance.

Process quality does not predict correctness

Trajectory scoring

Each cell was additionally scored against a rubric transferred from a document-AI verification standard: read the source before editing it, consult every declared source, do not silently repeat a discarded step. The deterministic postcondition remained the only gate — the process score never decides a cell.

A clean trajectory barely predicts passing: 60% against 56%. That is noise, and it is the correct outcome. The source rubric explicitly names “confusing process with outcome” as an error and rules that process is a tiebreaker, not a criterion. A process score that predicted outcome would have collapsed two axes that are deliberately separate.

Where it earns its place is exactly the tie above. On correctness the two leaders are indistinguishable; on process they are not, and the seat decision rests on evidence rather than preference.

Residency sampled throughout every epoch; zero CPU-spill samples across all packs.

Zero out-of-scope writes in 210 enforced cells. A negative result worth recording: this is not where these models fail.

×

The two remaining ties may be permanent.

An 11.3% per-cell noise floor bounds what any sample size can resolve. More trials cannot distinguish a genuine tie from insufficient data here.

×

This does not rank models above ~14B on easy work.

Six earlier fixtures are saturated — every model from 14B to 32B clears them. Those results say “nobody fails”, not “these are equal”.

×

Nothing here transfers to comprehension or judgment.

A separate 30-cell battery on long-document comprehension puts the capability floor at 3.4B — a model that scores 8/18 on this execution ladder. Execution and comprehension floors are different capabilities at different sizes.

×

Timings do not survive a hardware change.

Single RTX 3090 at a 320 W cap. A second card lands the day after this run; pass rates should transfer, wall-clock will not.

Practical reading

If you have to choose a local seat today

  • gemma4:26b is the executor seat — tied at the top, fastest of the leaders, perfectly stable, cleanest process.
  • Turn reasoning off for plan-shaped work. It costs nothing measurable in quality on tasks these models already solve and returns 5–32× in tokens.
  • Gate the unstable models rather than trusting a run. Stability is bimodal and predictable; treat a coin-flip model as a coin flip.
  • Do not buy parameters. A 12B beats a 33B here, and the 33B costs 20 GB to place sixth.
  • Nothing below ~12B for plan-shaped edit work — and note it fails by writing to a path that does not exist, not by refusing.

Reproduction

For the verifying reviewer

python3 evals/local_lane_ladder/runner.py \
  --models <models> \
  --tasks csv-summarize-repair strict-log-format ambiguous-anchor \
  --levels L2 --trials 12 \
  --num-ctx 16384 --temperature 0.8 --think off \
  --trace-dir <pack>/traces --state <pack>/state.json

Completeness gate — a short trace count means the pack is not scoreable: 

ls <pack>/traces/*.json | wc -l

Each pack retains per-cell JSON traces (raw stdout/stderr, argv, prompt, revision, parsed trajectory), state.json, a generated results matrix, a written finding, and evidence holding pre-run provenance and interval residency samples.

Prepared by the operator’s implementer seat. All figures are drawn from retained run artifacts rather than restated from narration.
